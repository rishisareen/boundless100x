What each metric measures

Analyst Coverage Count

How many analysts publish on the company. Fewer is better here — a stock that twenty desks already model is a stock already priced.

CAP Proxy (Consecutive yrs RoCE > CoE)

The longest unbroken run of years the business earned more than its cost of capital. A rough measure of how long the advantage actually lasted.

Capital Reinvestment Rate (ΔCapital / NOPAT)

How much of its after-tax operating profit the company ploughs back instead of paying out. High returns on capital only compound while the money stays in the business.

Cash Conversion (OCF/EBITDA)

How much of the reported operating profit actually arrived as cash, averaged over five years. Profit that never reaches the bank is the oldest warning sign there is.

DCF Margin of Safety

How far below the model's estimate of intrinsic value the shares trade. It is only ever as good as the growth and discount rate assumed underneath it.

Daily Turnover Ratio (%)

How much of the company changes hands on an average day. Too little and a position cannot be built or exited; too much and you are trading against a crowd.

Debt / Equity

How much the company has borrowed against every rupee of its own capital. Debt is what turns a bad year into a final one.

Dividend Payout Consistency

In how many of the last ten years the company paid a dividend at all. An unbroken record is evidence the reported profit was real cash.

DuPont: Asset Turnover

How many rupees of sales each rupee of assets produces. The second lever behind return on equity — how hard the asset base is worked.

DuPont: Equity Multiplier

How many rupees of assets sit on each rupee of equity. The third lever behind return on equity, and the only one that raises it by taking risk rather than by being good at something.

DuPont: Net Profit Margin

What is left of each rupee of sales after everything, in the latest year. The first of the three levers that together make up return on equity.

EBIT CAGR (3yr)

How fast operating profit compounded over the last three years, before interest and tax.

EBIT CAGR (5yr)

How fast operating profit compounded over five years, before interest and tax could flatter it or spoil it.

EPS CAGR (5yr)

How fast profit *per share* compounded over five years. This is the one an existing owner actually received, after any new shares were issued.

EV/EBITDA

What the whole company costs including its debt, against its pre-tax operating cash profit. The valuation multiple that does not care how the business is financed.

Earnings Yield Spread vs G-Sec

The earnings the company produces per rupee of price, minus what a ten-year government bond pays for no risk at all. It asks what the risk is buying you.

FCF Positive Count (10yr)

In how many of the last ten years the business generated more cash than it spent. It is the difference between a compounder and a story.

FCF Yield

The spare cash the business threw off last year, as a share of what the whole company costs to buy. It is what an owner could take out without shrinking it.

FII + DII Accumulation Streak

How many quarters in a row the funds have been adding. Two or more says somebody is buying before the accounts have explained why.

FII + DII Holding (%)

How much of the company professional funds already own. A little is validation that somebody serious has looked; a lot means the re-rating has happened without you.

Financial Leverage (avg)

How much faster earnings per share grow than operating profit. Near one is healthy; well above it means borrowed money is doing the work and will do the damage too.

Gross Margin Stability (10yr StdDev)

How much the operating margin swings across ten years. A margin that holds through a full cycle is the clearest evidence of pricing power there is.

Growth Quality Grade

Whether growth came from selling more at better prices, from fixed costs spreading over a bigger base, or from borrowing. Only the first two survive a downturn.

Interest Coverage

How many times over last year's operating profit covers the interest bill. Below three, a soft year starts eating the lenders' money rather than the owners'.

Market Cap (₹ Cr)

What the whole company costs today. Small is the point here — a hundred-bagger needs room to grow into, and a large cap has already used most of its room up.

Max Consecutive Growth Years (>10%)

The longest unbroken run of years sales grew more than 10%. Streaks say whether growth is a habit or an episode.

Operating Leverage (avg)

How much faster operating profit grows than sales. Above one, each extra rupee of sales brings more than its share of profit with it.

Operating Margin (5yr Avg)

What is left of each rupee of sales after running the business, averaged over five years. Wide margins that hold usually mean the company sets its own prices.

Owner-Operator Signal

Whether the people running the company own enough of it to think like owners. Founder-led with a large stake is the shape this model looks for.

P/E (TTM)

What the market pays for each rupee of the last year's profit. Cheap and dear only mean anything next to the sector and the company's own history.

P/E Percentile (10yr traded range)

Where today's P/E sits inside the range this company has actually traded at over ten years. It asks whether the stock is dear for itself, not for the market.

PAT CAGR (3yr)

How fast net profit compounded over the last three years — the recent slice of the five-year record, and where a slowdown appears first.

PAT CAGR (5yr)

How fast net profit compounded over five years. Faster than sales means margins widened; slower means something ate the gain on the way down.

PEG Ratio (5yr EPS, trailing)

The P/E divided by the five-year earnings growth rate. Around one, you are paying about a year of growth for a year of growth.

Promoter Holding Trend (5yr)

How much of the company the founding family still owns, latest quarter. The score reads the five-year change rather than the level: promoters selling down is the signal, not the number itself.

Promoter Pledge %

How much of the promoters' own stake is mortgaged to lenders. Pledged shares get sold in a falling market, by someone who is not the promoter; the implementation calls anything above 10% a red flag.

ROIIC (Return on Incremental Capital)

What the newest rupee of capital earned, rather than the whole base. Headline RoCE can stay high on old assets long after fresh money stops earning, and this is the number that fades first.

Re-rating Headroom (own-fundamentals justified multiple)

How far above today's traded multiple the company's own RoCE, growth and consistency would justify.

Real Revenue Growth (vs inflation)

Whether sales grew faster than inflation, and by how much. It cannot tell selling more from charging more — no filing this system reads reports unit volumes.

Reinvestment Rate (Capex/Depreciation)

What the company spends on assets against what its existing assets wear out by. Below one it is living off the base it already has.

Revenue CAGR (3yr)

How fast sales compounded over the last three years — the recent record, to be read against the five-year one beside it.

Revenue CAGR (5yr)

How fast sales compounded over five years. It is the top of the funnel — nothing below it grows for long without it.

Revenue Growth Consistency

How widely the year-on-year sales growth rate swings about. A steady grower is worth more than a faster one that keeps stopping.

Reverse DCF Implied Growth

The growth rate today's price already assumes, forever. The question it poses is whether the company has ever grown that fast; the flags answer it against the company's own delivered CAGR.
